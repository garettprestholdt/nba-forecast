How it works

The model

How the forecasts are made, how they were tested before going live, and how they're scored every morning since.

{% endblock %} {% block content %}

1 · Published forecast

Team ratings (Elo)

Each team has a rating that changes after every game, more when the result is a surprise or the margin is large. Ratings are shown in points: how much better than an average team on a neutral floor. Home court is estimated from recent seasons (currently {{ "%.1f" | format(home_court_points) }} points), and each summer ratings move {{ "%.0f" | format((1 - model_params.carryover) * 100) }}% back toward average.

2 · Challenger

Players and injuries

The challenger starts from the same ratings and adds the players: each one's box-score production and offensive and defensive impact, counting only players listed as available on that morning's injury report. It forecasts every game next to Elo, and replaces it only if it scores better over the full season, under a rule written before its first forecast.

3 · Record

Logged before tip-off

Each morning a scheduled job records last night's results, updates the ratings and logs today's forecasts with a timestamp. The log is append-only: a check rejects any change to a past forecast and any forecast made after tip-off. Finished games are scored against the betting market.

4 · Terms

Reading the numbers

Log-loss is the main accuracy measure. Lower is better, a coin flip scores 0.693, and confident misses cost the most. Calibration is whether 70% forecasts win about 70% of the time. Game Score sums up a player's box score in one number; about 10 is an average game.
